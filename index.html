Naghmeh Farzi

PhD Candidate in Computer Science · University of New Hampshire

I am a Computer Science PhD candidate at the University of New Hampshire, advised by Prof. Laura Dietz. My research sits at the intersection of Information Retrieval, Large Language Models (LLMs), and AI evaluation, with an interest in understanding and improving how AI systems behave, reason, and are evaluated. I am also interested in LLM architectures and internal model behavior, as well as the broader intersection of AI and social sciences. Before joining UNH, I received my master's degree in AI and Robotics from Shahid Beheshti University in Tehran, where I worked on machine reading comprehension and multiple-choice question answering in low-resource settings, focusing on Persian (Farsi). I received my bachelor's degree in Computer Science, specializing in Software Engineering, from Shiraz University. My undergraduate project involved developing a web application that used face detection to detect reader distraction and used environmental signals, such as noise and motion, to help redirect the reader's attention to the task.

Looking at my name, you might think it's long or hard to pronounce. It's actually just two syllables. The tricky part is the sounds, and since I'm not great with phonetics, I had to look it up. Apparently it's pronounced roughly /næɣme/. The "gh" is a sound that doesn't really exist in English, but is somewhat similar to the French "r."

Naghmeh (نغمه) is a Farsi word associated with music, sound, and song, and it comes originally from Arabic. Something I've found interesting is that when I say my name to people from different countries with a little vowel perturbation, they sometimes recognize it as a word in their own language with the same meaning. This has happened with Turkish, Hindi, and Arabic speakers, all from countries fairly close to Iran. Further west, the word doesn't seem to ring a bell. So as long as the name you say is close enough to my name, I'm fine with it. An easy way to say it is Nag-may: "Nag" with the "a" as in cat, then "may".

Funnily enough, back in my country people had trouble spelling my last name, and I got used to explaining it. We have several forms of "z" in my language, all pronounced the same. They're borrowed from Arabic, where they actually have different pronunciations. Also, in my language we don't write down short vowels (we only write them in the early years of elementary school), so with an unfamiliar word you're just relying on luck to get the short vowels right, and the "a" in my last name is one of them. I moved to the US as a certified expert in explaining my last name, only to find out it was my first name that needed explaining.
